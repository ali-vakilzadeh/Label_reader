Section 3: Sets (SetSize) — what one row means

One CSV row is one scanned article: one barcode, one packet, one photo session. A set of 2
stockings is a single article containing two garments, so it is ONE row with SetSize=2 — not
two rows, and not a row duplicated by cloning.

- SetSize is decided by the operator in the review dialog, after extraction. It is never sent
  to Gemini and never inferred from the photos: the packaging usually hides the second item,
  so a guess would be a confident wrong answer.
- Blank, absent, 0, 1 or any non-numeric value all mean 1. Importers must not fail a row on it.
- Netto and Brutto stay exactly as read from the label — the weight of the WHOLE packet,
  not per garment. The app does not divide by SetSize. A 2-pack labelled 100g exports as
  Netto=100g, SetSize=2, and 100g is the true weight of that row.
- SetSize is NOT the invoice quantity. The dashboard keeps its own Pieces column, which counts
  scanned articles (a parent plus its clones) and is not emitted by the app. A set of 2 is one
  article: Pieces=1, SetSize=2. Multiplying the two is the reader's decision, not the app's.
- All other fields describe the set as a whole. Two garments in one packet share one barcode,
  one size, one colour and one price; a packet of mixed sizes or colours is not a set and must
  be scanned as separate articles.

Section 4: Language - why this file is English

The operator may run the whole app in Armenian: Armenian labels on every AI result, Armenian
pickers for the choices they make. None of that reaches this file. Every column here carries the
canonical ENGLISH key, and that is deliberate.

What the app does:

  displays                    stores and exports here
  "Տաբատ"                     Trousers
  "Կապույտ - Մուգ"            Blue - Navy
  "Ամառ"                      Summer
  "Nike"                      Nike            (brand: English in both modes)

The Armenian the operator reads is a lookup on the device against the tables served by
GET /api/v1/reference-tables (api_contract.md v1.3 section 4.6), which carry
English key + Armenian label + numeric id. The app never translates, and neither does the AI.

Why it matters for this file:

- One garment type is ONE string. If the app wrote Armenian, the same trousers would arrive as
  "տաբատ", "Տաբատ" and "շալվար" on different rows, and no importer, filter or invoice total
  could group them. English keys are what make SubCategory searchable.
- The dashboard joins this file back to Armenian on its own tables when it renders a screen or
  builds an Armenian customs export. It needs a stable key to join ON, which is this column.
- Brand and Country are English even in Armenian output, by client decision (2026-08-30).
- Size and OriginalPrice are free text and are never translated in either direction.

Unmatched values: when the label said something outside the 295 sub-categories (or the brand,
country or material tables), the server returns the transcription unchanged and the app exports
that verbatim - still English, still one string. The dashboard flags it UNMATCHED and routes it
to review, where a supervisor decides its canonical English and its Armenian. Nothing is
silently corrected.

Importers must therefore:

- treat every column as English, and never attempt to detect or convert a language;
- accept a Brand, SubCategory, Country or Material value that is not in any reference table;
- match against the English column of middle_ware/reference_data/*.csv, case-insensitively.

Section 5: Vocabulary source

Brand, SubCategory, Country and Material come from tables that GROW; do not hardcode them.
The authoritative copies are middle_ware/reference_data/*.csv, which the middleware also serves
to the app. Category, Gender, Season and Color are short and fixed enough to hardcode for
validation, but their Armenian labels still come from the served tables.

| Column      | Table                | Rows | Armenian available |
|-------------|----------------------|------|--------------------|
| SubCategory | sub-category.csv     | 295  | yes                |
| Brand       | brand.csv            | 839  | no, English by decision |
| Country     | country.csv          | 222  | no, English by decision |
| Material    | material.csv         | 85   | yes                |
| Color       | color.csv            | 26   | yes                |
| Gender      | gender.csv           | 7    | yes                |
| Season      | season.csv           | 5    | yes                |
| Category    | (three fixed values) | 3    | yes, via the dashboard's category.csv |

A supervisor can add a row to any of these through the dashboard without an app release; the
app picks the change up from GET /api/v1/reference-tables. Rows are only ever added or given a
missing Armenian label - an English key is never renamed or deleted, because this file and every
export already made depend on it.
